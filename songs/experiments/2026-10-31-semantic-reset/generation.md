# Generation candidate — semantic reset

## Goal

One coherent song, not a bilingual exercise. The four target ideas must be understandable in Spanish and anchored naturally in French across the song.

Targets:
- añorar → regretter / avoir la nostalgie de
- sosiego → calme / quiétude
- hallar → trouver
- dejar atrás → laisser derrière soi

## Lyrics

[Verse]
Dejo atrás la estación y su ruido al cerrar,
añoro aquellas mañanas que no van a regresar.
Busco sosiego en un café casi vacío,
hasta hallar tu carta dentro de mi abrigo.

[Pre-Chorus]
La leo despacio, ya no quiero volver,
afuera amanece y empiezo a entender.

[French Break]
Je regrette encore nos matins d'avant,
mais je laisse cette histoire derrière moi.
Dans le café vide, je retrouve enfin le calme,
et je trouve ta lettre au fond de mon manteau.

[Chorus]
Dejo atrás lo que pesa, lo dejo caer,
añoro lo vivido sin quererlo retener.
Hay sosiego en la calle cuando empieza a llover,
y hallo un poco de aire para volver a crecer.

[Outro]
No hace falta olvidar para poder avanzar.

## Semantic check

- **añorar**: Spanish explicitly misses past mornings; French says "Je regrette encore nos matins d'avant."
- **dejar atrás**: Spanish leaves the station / burden behind; French says "je laisse cette histoire derrière moi."
- **sosiego**: Spanish searches for calm in the café; French says "je retrouve enfin le calme."
- **hallar**: Spanish finds the letter; French says "je trouve ta lettre."

The French passage anchors all four targets, but the rest remains a continuous song.

## Suno style prompt

Dark intimate Spanish acoustic-indie song, raw and restrained, around 1 minute. Close natural lead vocal with clear native-sounding Spanish and French pronunciation. Dry nylon/acoustic guitar, low warm bass, sparse percussion only if needed, subtle room ambience. Emotionally tense and direct rather than sweet.

Avoid cute, twee, cozy, bubbly or whimsical production. No bouncy kick-snare pulse, no "boom boom" soft-pop beat, no ukulele-like plucks, no sugary chorus, no educational-song feel.

Keep a coherent narrative and natural phrasing. Spanish dominates. The French break should feel like a real lyrical continuation of the same scene, not a lesson or glossary. Leave a short musical breath before and after the French passage. Preserve intelligibility without making the whole track artificially slow.
