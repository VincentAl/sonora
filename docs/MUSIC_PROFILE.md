## Strongest inferred preferences

### 1. Intimacy over spectacle

Prefer close, human, emotionally direct performances over large cinematic production.

Useful traits:
- close-mic vocal;
- restrained dynamics;
- audible breathing room;
- organic imperfections;
- sparse arrangements;
- emotional tension without belting.

### 2. Acoustic / organic core

A recurring axis is voice + guitar / piano / organic accompaniment rather than dense electronic production.

Useful palette:
- nylon or acoustic guitar;
- dry piano;
- subtle strings only when necessary;
- hand percussion;
- light bass;
- room ambience rather than huge reverbs.

### 3. Melancholic, vulnerable writing

The target mood is often intimate, bittersweet, fragile, wounded, sensual, or quietly intense.

Avoid:
- cheerful educational-song energy;
- cartoonish positivity;
- motivational-pop uplift;
- oversized anthemic choruses.

### 4. Spanish and Iberian / Latin texture

The references tolerate a wide range inside Spanish-language music:
- Latin indie folk;
- singer-songwriter;
- acoustic pop;
- contemporary Spanish indie;
- restrained flamenco / folk influence;
- raw regional or traditional texture.

This is broader than generic "Latin pop".

### 5. Modern production, but not polished to death

Even when electronic elements appear, they should support the vocal and leave space.

Useful electronic traits:
- soft sub-bass;
- minimal beat;
- muted synth beds;
- textural percussion;
- low-density arrangement.

## Revised generation families

The original three families remain useful, but future experiments should prioritize these more specific profiles.

### A. Bare intimate acoustic

Voice-forward, nylon/acoustic guitar, almost no percussion, very close and vulnerable.

Use for:
- difficult new vocabulary;
- French anchoring experiments;
- pronunciation-sensitive songs.

### B. Dark indie acoustic-pop

Acoustic foundation plus restrained modern bass/drums, melancholic hook, slightly more memorable chorus.

Use for:
- repeat-listening potential;
- stronger hooks without losing intelligibility.

### C. Raw Iberian / flamenco-adjacent

Dry guitar, hand percussion or palmas-like rhythmic texture used sparingly, raw vocal phrasing, earthy timbre.

Do not imitate a named flamenco performer or existing song.

Use for:
- rhythmically memorable vocabulary;
- expressive Spanish phrasing.

### D. Minimal nocturnal electronic

Sparse beat, warm sub-bass, airy/dark synth texture, intimate vocal, low stimulation.

Use for:
- work-background listening;
- testing whether electronic repetition improves retention.

### E. Fragile piano / chamber folk

Piano-led, very light acoustic or string texture, slow phrasing, emotionally exposed.

Use for:
- semantic-anchor experiments;
- passages where French needs maximum intelligibility.

## Prompt-writing rules

When generating prompts:

- describe musical properties, not living artists;
- prioritize intimate, restrained, melancholic, voice-forward production;
- keep the arrangement sparse enough that Spanish and French remain intelligible;
- allow mild regional / folk texture rather than forcing generic Latin-pop clichés;
- use female or male vocal as an experiment variable rather than a fixed rule;
- keep bilingual transitions spacious;
- avoid copying recognizable melodies, arrangements, or vocal identities.

## Current hypothesis

The best Sonora songs are likely to sit closer to intimate acoustic / dark indie / raw Iberian textures than to generic pop.

The previous "minimal Latin/electronic" family remains worth testing, but should be treated as one branch rather than the default aesthetic.
